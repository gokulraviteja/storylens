# Audio, Editing, Captions, and Export Standard

## Editing principle

The edit should continuously guide attention toward the current idea. Frequent cutting is useful only when the post-cut image is simple, relevant, and connected to what came before.

## Visual rotation

Avoid long runs of similar wide illustrations. Rotate among:

- Wide environment
- Character close-up or reaction
- Object detail
- First-person view
- Map or route
- Timeline
- Diagram or process
- Before/after comparison
- Evidence-versus-reconstruction card
- Comedic metaphor
- Quiet atmospheric shot

Most frames should communicate one dominant idea and have one clear focal point.

## Limited-motion vocabulary

Prefer motivated movement:

- Pose or expression changes
- Hand and object movement
- Fire, smoke, rain, water, dust, mist, and leaves
- Foreground/background parallax
- Diagram construction and highlighting
- Route drawing and scale comparison
- Push-in toward a newly important detail

Do not use repeated slow zooms as a substitute for new visual information.

## Voice production

- Produce narration in performance sections rather than one undirected full-script take.
- Give each section an intention: urgency, curiosity, amusement, explanation, tension, or reflection.
- Generate or record alternatives for important lines.
- Evaluate by listening without the script.
- Check pronunciation, emphasis, pauses, pitch variation, and sentence endings.
- Never imitate a real person's voice without permission.

## Sound hierarchy

1. Narration
2. Story-relevant effects
3. Environmental ambience
4. Music

Music should change with the story's sections instead of looping unchanged. Use silence or reduced ambience before important reveals. Effects should be synchronized with visible events rather than added as decoration.

### Working mix targets

- Integrated loudness: approximately -14 to -16 LUFS
- True peak: no higher than -1 dBTP
- Music generally 8–12 dB below narration, adjusted by ear
- Duck music under speech
- Test on headphones, laptop speakers, and a phone speaker
- Check mono compatibility

## Captions

Maintain two layers when possible:

- Accurate YouTube closed captions for accessibility and translation
- Selective designed captions for emphasis

Designed captions should use one or two lines, natural phrase breaks, high contrast, safe margins, and restrained highlighting. Avoid covering the focal action or forcing the viewer to read every word while also processing a complex frame.

## Color and readability

- Use a limited video palette with one story accent.
- Reduce background detail and contrast behind the focal subject.
- Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
- Maintain at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for sufficiently large text.

## Technical export

Default long-form YouTube master:

- MP4 container with fast start
- H.264 High Profile, progressive, 4:2:0
- 16:9 at 1920×1080 or higher
- 24, 25, or 30 fps, matching the project timeline
- BT.709 SDR color metadata
- AAC-LC stereo at 48 kHz
- Accurate subtitle file exported separately

Upload unlisted first and check YouTube's processed result on desktop and mobile before publication.
