## Purpose

This is the channel-level idea backlog. Concepts remain here while they are being compared. Once an idea is approved, create a separate video project and maintain all research, scripts, visuals, audio, and production decisions inside that project.

## Status vocabulary

- **Draft:** early concept requiring definition
- **Candidate:** promising enough for preliminary research
- **Selected:** approved to become a video project
- **In production:** active project exists
- **Published:** released and reviewed
- **Parked:** retained but not currently prioritized
- **Rejected:** deliberately removed from consideration, with a reason

## Current ranking

### 1. How Generational Wealth Began

**Status:** Selected for concept development; geography and historical mechanism not yet locked.

**One-line concept:** How did a small number of families acquire acres of land while many others owned little or none—and how did that first advantage compound across generations?

**Why it ranks first:**

- Personally relevant to a broad audience
- Natural mystery involving land, power, inheritance, and inequality
- Strong emotional and discussion potential
- Can expand into a connected series across countries and historical systems

**Primary risk:** “Generational wealth” is too broad for one Short. The first video must choose one geography, period, and land-allocation mechanism. It must avoid implying that every large estate arose in the same way.

**Possible mechanisms to investigate:**

- Grants from rulers
- Military or administrative rewards
- Colonial land settlements
- Conversion of commonly used land into private title
- Auctions, taxation, debt, and dispossession
- Inheritance and later appreciation of land values

**Recommended next decision:** India versus a global comparison. Recommendation: begin with one Indian regional system rather than attempting all of India in one minute.

**Status:** Candidate; project shell prepared.

**Why it ranks second:**

### 3. Archaeology's Unfair Archive

**Status:** Candidate; project shell prepared.

**One-line concept:** Why do stone tools survive for thousands of years while baskets, bindings, handles, meals, and much of ordinary life disappear?

**Why it ranks third:**

- Memorable framing and strong observational humor
- Explains StoryLens's evidence-versus-reconstruction philosophy

**Primary risk:** Organic material can survive in special conditions. Present this as a preservation bias, not an absolute rule.

### 4. The Five-Kilometre Clue

**Status:** Candidate; project shell prepared.

**One-line concept:** How can one small piece of stone reveal that prehistoric people knew and moved through a landscape much larger than one rock shelter?

**Why it ranks fourth:**

## Idea selection criteria

Score future concepts on:

1. Immediate hook
2. Personal or emotional relevance
3. One clear central question
4. Surprising but defensible payoff
5. Visual potential
6. Suitability for the intended duration
7. Reliable source availability
8. Expansion potential into long-form or a series
9. Production feasibility
10. Fit with StoryLens's channel promise

High audience potential cannot compensate for a misleading premise. Easy production cannot compensate for an uninteresting payoff.
