# StoryLens

## Core idea

StoryLens is a YouTube storytelling project that explains fascinating stories and events through illustrated visuals, narration, music, and sound design.

The channel will cover stories from both the distant past and the present. Videos should feel like visual documentaries or illustrated reconstructions rather than static AI-image slideshows.

**Working promise:** Remarkable stories, explained visually.

## Languages

The content may be produced in:

- English
- Hindi
- Telugu

Each language will be released as a separate rendered video with its own narration, subtitles, title, description, and culturally natural script adaptation. The Hindi and Telugu scripts should not be literal word-for-word translations.

The initial focus is the English version. The same research, scene plan, and visual assets can later be reused for the Hindi and Telugu versions.

## Content pillars

### Ancient worlds

- How ancient people lived
- Civilizations, rulers, wars, cities, trade, food, and daily life
- Historical incidents and forgotten stories
- Archaeological discoveries

### Mythology and folklore

- Stories from Indian and world mythology
- Origins and interpretations of legends
- Comparisons between literary tradition and historical evidence
- Clear labeling of mythology, folklore, and established history

### Ideas that changed us

- Inventions and discoveries
- How familiar objects and systems developed
- The people, problems, and accidents behind important innovations

### Disasters decoded

- Plane crashes and other transport accidents
- Engineering failures and natural disasters
- Step-by-step timelines based on authoritative reports
- Clear separation between confirmed findings, preliminary information, and speculation

### Cases unfolded

- Carefully researched criminal cases and investigations
- Completed or well-documented cases are preferred initially
- Court records, official statements, and reputable reporting should be prioritized
- Allegations must never be presented as proven facts

## Storytelling approach

Each video should tell a story rather than simply list facts.

A typical video structure:

1. Open with an immediate question, surprising moment, or immersive scenario.
2. Establish the time, place, people, and central conflict.
3. Reconstruct the event in a clear sequence.
4. Introduce historical, scientific, or investigative context naturally.
5. Reveal an important detail, misconception, or turning point.
6. End with a memorable conclusion or unanswered question.

For historical daily-life videos, the viewer can be placed inside the world: for example, “You wake up in Mohenjo-daro, 4,000 years ago.”

## Visual identity

The preferred look is non-photorealistic illustrated storytelling:

- Hand-drawn cinematic storybook art
- Ink outlines and textured color
- Painterly light and atmosphere
- Expressive characters
- Stylized maps, diagrams, and timelines
- Occasional simple animation for technical explanations

The project should develop an original house style instead of describing or marketing the visuals as the style of a particular living artist or animation studio.

AI-generated pictures can be enhanced with:

- Slow pans and zooms
- Layered parallax
- Light, smoke, dust, rain, or environmental movement
- Maps and route animations
- Labels and restrained on-screen text
- Ambient sound, music, and carefully placed sound effects

When an AI image reconstructs a recent real event and could be mistaken for genuine footage, it should be visibly identified as a **visual reconstruction**.

## Production workflow

1. Select a topic and define the central question.
2. Gather reliable sources and create a research brief.
3. Write and fact-check the English master script.
4. Record or generate the English voice-over, initially using an authorized ElevenLabs voice.
5. Divide the narration into scenes.
6. Write consistent visual prompts and generate the illustrations.
7. Build the English edit with animation, captions, music, and sound design.
8. Adapt the script for Hindi and Telugu.
9. Record the additional voice-overs using authorized dubbing artists or properly licensed voices.
10. Duplicate and retime the edit for each language.
11. Create language-specific titles, descriptions, captions, and thumbnails.
12. Review facts, pronunciation, audio levels, visual continuity, and policy risks before publishing.

## Editing and automation

The first two or three pilot videos should be edited manually in software such as DaVinci Resolve, Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro, or CapCut. This will help establish the pacing and visual language before investing in automation.

Remotion or a similar code-driven workflow may be introduced later. A future automated video could be defined with structured scene data containing:

- Illustration
- Start and end time
- Camera movement
- Narration
- Subtitle
- On-screen text
- Music cue
- Sound effect

## Editorial standards

- Accuracy and clarity are more important than speed.
- Cite authoritative sources in the description where appropriate.
- Clearly distinguish fact, expert interpretation, mythology, allegation, and speculation.
- Do not invent dialogue and present it as historical fact.
- Avoid graphic imagery, gore, or thumbnails designed to shock.
- Treat victims and surviving family members with dignity.
- Avoid unnecessary use of private individuals' names or likenesses.
- Do not imitate a real person's voice without explicit permission.
- For ongoing investigations, state what is known, what is alleged, and what remains unknown.

## Suggested pilot

**Working title:** You Wake Up in the Stone Age During the Monsoon

The pilot can be a 4–5 minute, 16:9 video using approximately 20–25 illustrated scenes. It follows a small Mesolithic hunter-gatherer group in central India through one monsoon day. The story can explore shelter, fire, food, travel, insects, contaminated water, predators, and the ways seasonal rain renewed the landscape.

Because prehistoric people left no written accounts, the video must distinguish archaeological evidence and scientific interpretation from plausible visual reconstruction.

Pilot deliverables:

- Source and research brief
- English narration script
- Scene-by-scene storyboard
- AI visual prompts
- Voice direction
- Music and sound plan
- Editing timeline
- Subtitle file
- Thumbnail concepts
- Hindi and Telugu adaptations after the English format is approved

The repeatable production system is defined in the [video-project workflow](../framework/video-project-workflow.md).

## Initial success criteria

The pilot should answer these questions:

- Does the opening create curiosity immediately?
- Does the narration sound human and emotionally appropriate?
- Do the illustrations remain visually consistent?
- Does the video feel alive rather than like a slideshow?
- Is the information trustworthy and easy to follow?
- Can the workflow be repeated without sacrificing quality?
