# StoryLens Channel Style Bible

## Status

**Image-only base-frame rule locked on 2026-09-06.** The permanent illustration treatment remains experimental, but code-drawn scenes and diagrams are no longer permitted.

## Mandatory image-only rule

Every visual beat must use a generated or human-illustrated image as its base. Python, SVG, canvas, presentation software, or other code must not draw the scene, character, object, icon, map, timeline, anatomical graphic, or diagram.

Code may be used only to assemble and finish approved images: crop, scale, layer, mask, animate, transition, colour-correct, synchronize, mix audio, and add captions or short evidence/reconstruction labels. Editorial text overlays are not a substitute for the required image.

For every video project:

- generate a distinct image for each materially different visual beat;
- evaluate every image before it enters the timeline;
- regenerate failed anatomy, continuity, historical detail, composition, or artificial-looking results;
- never replace a difficult image with a code-drawn visual shortcut;
- reduce the number of beats or shorten the video if the available image set cannot sustain the quality standard.

## Purpose

All StoryLens videos should feel as if they belong to the same channel, even when the era, geography, weather, or subject changes. Consistency should come from a small set of recognizable rules rather than making every video look identical.

## Fixed channel-level system

These elements should remain consistent across video projects once approved:

- Illustration family and treatment of outlines, texture, light, and faces
- Typography family and hierarchy
- Title-card and location/date-card design
- Maps, timelines, diagrams, labels, and reconstruction disclosures
- Subtitle styling and safe-area rules
- Camera-movement vocabulary
- Transition vocabulary
- Narration character and pacing range
- Music philosophy and sound-design density
- Thumbnail composition grammar, typography, and logo placement
- Rules for historical uncertainty and visual reconstructions

## Flexible video-level system

These elements can change to serve each story:

- Characters and material culture
- Geography, architecture, vegetation, and weather
- Dominant color palette and lighting conditions
- Music instrumentation and environmental sound
- Diagram content
- Exact thumbnail scene and accent color

## Experimental visual foundation

The current StoryLens production target is a **minimalist 2D historical explainer with occasional cinematic story shots**. It combines the clarity, pace, and production economy of hand-drawn web animation with StoryLens's stronger research, atmosphere, and respect for historical people.

Project 007 also tests a **dark illuminated-manuscript treatment**: near-black vellum, etched ink, oxblood shadows, tarnished-gold highlights, serif captions and low-key lighting. When used for prehistoric subjects, this is an aesthetic treatment only; it must not introduce medieval clothing, architecture, writing or objects. Keep this variant experimental until creator and audience comparison determines whether its mystery improves retention enough to justify its reduced brightness and slower pace.

The format mix should be approximately:

- 70% simple narration-led explainer shots
- 20% atmospheric or emotionally immersive story shots
- 10% maps, timelines, evidence, or reconstruction diagrams

This ratio is a starting hypothesis. Audience retention and production effort from the pilot will determine whether it becomes the permanent house system.

### Character design

- Highly simplified, reusable human proportions
- Thick, slightly irregular black outlines that retain a hand-drawn quality
- Simple, highly readable faces and gestures
- Controlled comedy exaggeration when it helps communicate an idea
- Flat color shapes with no realistic skin texture
- No shading in most explainer shots; at most one hard-edged shadow tone in cinematic shots
- Reusable character model sheets for proportions, expressions, clothing, and key poses
- Respectful, capable portrayals rather than historical or prehistoric caricatures

### Environment design

- White, single-color, or extremely sparse backgrounds for explanation
- Simplified layered environments only when place, weather, or danger matters to the story
- Large readable shapes instead of dense surface detail
- Historically important objects receive enough detail to be identifiable; decorative detail remains restrained
- Each video uses an earth-derived limited palette with one strong story-specific accent
- Atmosphere comes primarily from composition, color, sound, and motion rather than photorealistic rendering

### Motion language

- One narration idea per visual composition
- A meaningful visual change approximately every 3–5 seconds; faster when the narration introduces a quick sequence of ideas
- Limited character animation focused on gestures, expressions, hands, posture, and interaction with objects
- Direct visual metaphors and simple diagrams for abstract ideas, created as evaluated images rather than code drawings
- Layered parallax and restrained camera movement only when they add atmosphere or spatial understanding
- Environmental motion such as rain, smoke, fire, water, dust, mist, and leaves
- Clean image-generated or human-illustrated maps and diagrams using the same outline weight, simplified shapes, and palette system
- Prefer object, character, fire, smoke, rain, or diagram animation over camera zooms on static images
- Stillness may be used deliberately for emphasis, but a shot should not remain unchanged while the narration introduces several new ideas

### Narration-to-visual rule

Before final art is produced, every sentence or clause in the narration must be mapped to a visible action, object, expression, comparison, map, or diagram. If the viewer must rely on subtitles to understand an idea that could be shown, the shot should be redesigned.

The reference format's success is treated as evidence for this clarity and pacing model, not proof that any particular drawing style caused its view count. StoryLens should adapt the mechanics without copying another channel's characters, compositions, wording, jokes, or branding.

### Avoid

- Photorealism or near-photorealistic skin
- Dense painterly texture and cinematic micro-detail
- Airbrushed gradients and glossy 3D rendering
- Generic AI-fantasy lighting
- Overdesigned clothing, props, jewelry, and backgrounds
- Constant jokes that weaken danger, dignity, or factual accuracy
- Holding one detailed generated image under multiple narration ideas
- Using slow zooms and pans as the main source of motion
- Copying the recognizable style of a named artist, show, film, or studio
- Code-generated scenes, icons, maps, timelines, anatomy, or diagrams

The style should feel intentionally designed and repeatable, not like a sequence of unrelated generated illustrations.

## Proposed thumbnail grammar

- One clear human subject or human-scale action
- One immediately understandable problem
- Strong warm/cool or light/dark separation
- Simple background silhouette
- Zero to four words of text
- Text should add information rather than repeat the title
- No collage of many unrelated elements
- No misleading object, emotion, or event
- Must remain understandable when displayed at mobile size

## Pilot application

For the revised Stone Age monsoon pilot:

- Base palette: wet stone, forest green, earth brown, and blue-grey rain
- Story accent: ember orange
- Central visual motif: protected warmth surrounded by water and darkness
- Environmental motion: rain sheets, dripping rock, smoke, flame, leaves, mist, and moving surface water
- Thumbnail premise: a person shielding a fragile ember beneath a rock shelter
- Rendering: thick imperfect outlines, flat shapes, very simple reusable anatomy, sparse explainer backgrounds, and no shading except in selected atmospheric inserts
- Shot cadence: approximately 6–9 distinct visual ideas in a 25–30 second sample
- Humor: observational and lightly modern in the narration, never based on portraying prehistoric people as stupid
- Captions: accurate and readable, but smaller and less visually dominant than in the first sample

The revised sample must be compared with the first sample for immediate clarity, human-authored feel, narration–visual synchronization, pacing, atmosphere, historical credibility, and production effort. The permanent style remains an audience-tested decision.
