# StoryLens Narration Evaluation Gate

## Purpose

No script advances to final voice production merely because it is accurate or grammatically polished. It must sound as if a curious human is telling the viewer something worth hearing.

The evaluator reviews the words independently before judging the voice actor or synthetic voice. A strong performance cannot repair weak writing.

## Hard failures

Revise the script before production if any of these are true:

- The first two sentences provide only background and no curiosity, consequence, contrast, or surprise.
- The narration claims an unsupported historical fact or hides reconstruction as certainty.
- The intended second-person perspective disappears from the opening.
- Multiple consecutive sentences use abstract documentary language that people rarely say aloud.
- The script explains information that the planned visual already communicates without adding meaning.
- Humor depends on treating historical people as stupid, primitive, or incompetent.
- The voice is interchangeable with a generic history channel and contains no recognizable StoryLens personality.

## Scored review

Score each category from 0 to 2.

1. **Hook:** Does the opening earn attention immediately?
2. **Curiosity:** Does each section create a reason to hear the next line?
3. **Conversational voice:** Would a natural speaker plausibly use these words?
4. **Specificity:** Are claims expressed through concrete objects, actions, or consequences?
5. **Rhythm:** Is there useful variation in sentence length, emphasis, and pace?
6. **Visual actionability:** Can each meaningful clause produce a clear visual response?
7. **Personality and humor:** Is there an authored point of view without forced jokes?
8. **Historical responsibility:** Are accuracy, dignity, and uncertainty preserved?

Maximum score: 16.

- **Pass:** 14–16, with no hard failure
- **Revise:** 10–13, or any correctable hard failure
- **Reject:** below 10 or a fundamentally unsuitable voice

## AI-sounding-language pass

Flag and usually rewrite:

- Empty scene-setting such as “in a world where” or “a landscape transformed by”
- Repeated dramatic fragments used without purpose
- Grand conclusions unsupported by the preceding detail
- Symmetrical three-part phrases appearing repeatedly
- Excessive adjectives, atmospheric filler, and vague stakes
- Formal wording where contractions or ordinary verbs would sound more natural
- Generic transitions such as “But little did they know,” “This was no ordinary,” or “What happened next”

These patterns are not forbidden individually. The evaluator should judge whether they sound earned and specific in context.

## Listening test

Before approval:

1. Record a scratch performance at the intended pace.
2. Listen once without reading the script.
3. Mark the first moment attention drifts.
4. Ask what concrete image remained in memory.
5. Rewrite any passage that needs subtitles to feel interesting.
6. Test the opening on at least three people who have not seen the outline.

## Evaluation record

For each script version, record:

- Version and date
- Estimated and measured duration
- Reading speed in words per minute
- Eight category scores
- Hard failures
- Lines that feel written rather than spoken
- First attention-drop point
- Pass, Revise, or Reject decision
- Required changes
