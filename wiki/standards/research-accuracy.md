# Research and Historical Responsibility Standard

## Evidence classes

Every material claim used in a video project must be classified as:

- **Confirmed:** directly supported by strong evidence
- **Interpreted:** supported by a defensible scholarly interpretation
- **Reconstructed:** plausible narrative detail not directly known
- **Avoid:** unsupported, misleading, excessively disputed, or unnecessary

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. Primary archaeological reports, official investigations, court records, datasets, and original documents
2. Peer-reviewed research and authoritative institutional syntheses
3. Museums, universities, government agencies, and recognized subject experts
4. High-quality secondary reporting

Do not use search summaries, copied listicles, or unsourced social posts as evidence.

## Claim ledger

Each video project maintains its own claim ledger containing:

- Claim identifier
- Exact claim
- Evidence class
- Source and locator
- Confidence and disagreement
- Permitted narration wording
- Visual implications
- Prohibited overstatement

## Reconstruction rules

- Do not invent dialogue and present it as historical fact.
- Do not show a specific tool, food, species, ritual, relationship, or technique when the evidence supports only a broader category.
- Label visual reconstruction when a viewer could mistake it for documented evidence or real footage.
- Humor may target modern assumptions but must not turn ancient people, victims, or private individuals into caricatures.
- If a factual correction changes the story after research lock, reopen every affected downstream gate.

## Research lock

Research passes only when the central story does not depend on an unsupported claim, important uncertainty is visible, and every factual narration line can be traced to the claim ledger.
