# Shorts Batch 001

Status: Four production-ready English Shorts completed; creator review and audience publication pending.

## Experiment objective

Test StoryLens's human-sounding narration, fast visual storytelling, minimalist historical art, and limited animation through six independent 35–60 second videos before producing another long video.

## Production order

### 002 — How a tax rule helped build inherited land wealth

Core question: How could a colonial revenue rule help turn control over land revenue into property that accumulated across generations?

Evidence anchor: The Permanent Settlement regulation fixed the state revenue demand, secured estates at that assessment for heirs and lawful successors, permitted sale or gift, and required sale for arrears.

Visual opportunity: a fixed Company payment line, rising rent/cultivation above it, an inheritance handoff, and an auction reversal.

Recommendation: Produce first. It combines personal relevance, power, inheritance, and a surprising mechanism, while creating a strong correction to the simplistic “the British gave away free land” story.

### 003 — The tiny Stone Age tools that changed everything

Core question: Why would skilled toolmakers deliberately make blades smaller than a finger?

Evidence anchor: Mesolithic layers at Bhimbetka contained geometric microliths including backed blades, triangles, trapezes, and crescents.

Visual opportunity: immediate scale comparison, multiple shapes, reconstructed hafting, close-up transformation from tiny stone to useful cutting edge.

Recommendation: Produce first. It has a concrete object, a strong misconception, a satisfying visual transformation, and limited reconstruction risk.

### 004 — Archaeology's unfair filing system

Core question: Why do we know prehistoric stone tools better than the baskets, mats, handles, and meals around them?

Evidence anchor: durable stone survives while most organic material decays; reconstructed uses must remain distinct from excavated objects.

Visual opportunity: split-screen survival race between stone and organic objects, comedic archive metaphor, evidence-versus-reconstruction payoff.

Recommendation: Produce second. It tests StoryLens humor and research disclosure without turning into a disclaimer.

### 005 — A five-kilometre Stone Age clue

Core question: Why does one small piece of stone reveal a much larger human landscape?

Evidence anchor: an Archaeological Survey of India report traced some Bhimbetka siliceous material to a source near Barkhera, roughly 5 km away.

Visual opportunity: hand-held stone close-up, map reveal, walking-scale comparison, carried-tool payoff.

Recommendation: Produce third. It tests maps, scale, and a less character-driven visual format.

## Reserve concepts

- Why Stone Age people used rock shelters rather than living deep inside “caves”
- What a grinding stone can reveal—and what it cannot tell us about dinner
- Why protecting an existing fire in rain does not mean people were unable to make fire

## Test design

Keep the channel promise constant while varying one main presentation variable:

| Project | Primary test |
|---|---|
| 002 Permanent Settlement | Personal relevance, mechanism clarity, and controversy-safe framing |
| 003 Microliths | Object transformation and visual speed |
| 004 Archive | Humor and evidence disclosure |
| 005 Five-kilometre stone | Map, scale, and curiosity payoff |

Do not declare a winner from raw views alone. Compare engaged viewing, retention, response quality, production effort, and suitability for long-form expansion.
