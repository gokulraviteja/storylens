# StoryLens Shorts Strategy

Status: Locked for the first channel experiment on 2026-09-01.

## Strategic role

StoryLens remains a long-form storytelling concept. Shorts are the rapid-learning and discovery layer used to validate topics, hooks, narration, visual language, animation, and sound before committing to full videos.

Shorts are not chopped trailers and are not a separate creative identity. Each Short must deliver a complete idea while remaining compatible with the channel's long-form promise.

## Initial experiment

- Produce six standalone Shorts.
- Choose duration from the idea rather than forcing every Short toward one minute. The initial working range is 25–60 seconds, including focused 25–35 second Shorts.
- Treat every Short as its own video project.
- Publish no faster than the team can maintain the quality standard.
- Use the same broad audience, historical responsibility, and visual identity across Shorts and long-form videos.
- After six releases, choose the strongest topic-format combination for a 4–6 minute video.

## Short structure

### 0–2 seconds — stop the swipe

- Begin on a concrete object, action, discomfort, or contradiction.
- Put the central visual on screen immediately.
- Do not begin with a logo, greeting, date card, or broad setup.

### 2–10 seconds — establish the question

- Tell viewers exactly what is strange or consequential.
- Confirm the promise made by the first frame and title.

### Middle — escalate through evidence

- Introduce only the facts needed for this one idea.
- Use a meaningful visual change approximately every 1.5–3 seconds.
- Alternate literal scenes, object close-ups, diagrams, comparisons, and reactions.

### Final 5–10 seconds — payoff

- Answer or reframe the opening question.
- End on the strongest idea or image.
- Do not add a generic “like and subscribe” line that weakens the ending.

## Script standard

- Approximately 65–95 words for a 25–35 second Short and 100–155 words for a 45–60 second Short, adjusted after an actual narration timing test
- One central question
- One surprising turn
- One clear payoff
- Every line must advance, clarify, surprise, amuse, visualize, or pay off
- Conversational language, varied sentence length, and directed performance
- No compressed miniature lecture

## Visual standard

- Vertical 9:16 composition designed natively for mobile
- Approximately 10–16 meaningful image beats in 25–35 seconds or 15–25 beats in 45–60 seconds
- One focal point per beat
- Large objects, faces, hands, diagrams, and readable silhouettes
- Text kept within safe areas and away from interface overlays
- Every beat begins with an evaluated generated or human-illustrated image; code-drawn visuals are prohibited
- Limited animation through alternate generated poses, image-layer movement, environmental motion, and motivated reframing
- No repeated slow zooms over static generated images

## Sound standard

- Final-quality directed narration before visual production
- Music with at least one purposeful change or reduction
- Story-relevant effects synchronized to visible actions
- Silence or reduced sound before the key reveal when useful
- Voice tested on phone speakers

## Measurement

Do not rank Shorts by raw views alone. Since March 2025, a public Shorts view can count when playback starts or replays; YouTube retains **Engaged views** to show viewers who chose to continue watching.

Track:

- Shown in feed
- How many chose to view versus swiped away
- Engaged views
- Audience retention and completion
- Rewatches
- Likes, comments, shares, and subscribers gained
- Production time and revision count
- Whether the Short reveals a viable long-form question

Compare Shorts only with other Shorts, not with long-form metrics.

## Connection to long-form

When a related long-form video exists, attach it using YouTube's Related Video feature. URLs in Shorts descriptions and comments are not clickable, so they are not a reliable conversion path.
